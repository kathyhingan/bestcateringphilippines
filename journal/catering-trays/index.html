What catering trays
actually cover.

Start here

Catering trays are the shared containers that move food from a caterer's kitchen to your table, and in the Philippines they carry most of the celebrations that run without a service crew: christenings, office lunches, seminars, wakes, reunions and the long handaan table at Christmas. Catering trays of food arrive set on the table with utensils, guests serve themselves, and the caterer plans the tray count, the transport and the timing instead of assigning servers.

When does a tray set up make sense? Three honest answers: when the venue has no kitchen access, when the celebration eats across several hours without a program that seats everyone at once, and when the budget should feed people instead of paying for staff. When the event is a plated reception or a program with a sit-down course, the trays step back and the buffet or plated service carries the meal; the tray line-up still shows up, smaller, at the merienda table or the takeaway counter.

The rest of this guide walks the tray formats, the materials, the chafing dishes behind hot buffets, and the quote lines each one carries, so you can order the tray line-up with the rest of the food in one place.

The tray line-up:
formats and materials.

Pick the tray

The tray family splits by job. Catering food trays are the large shared containers, usually good for 8 to 12 people as one dish beside rice and viands. Catering serving trays are the smaller platters that carry puto, kakanin, sandwiches or fruit at the side of the same table. Catering party trays are the small-format versions that anchor a gathering on their own, one tray per 10 to 15 guests for snack courses, dessert rounds or the pulutan table at a Christmas party.

Material follows the job. Catering foil trays are the single-event workhorses: light, oven-safe and disposable, which is why menudo, pancit and lechon paksiw travel in them. Plastic catering trays carry packed meal drops and feeding programs where the tray never comes back. Silver catering trays dress a head table or a debut spread, and stainless catering trays are the inventory a caterer buys once; the same sheet metal line is sold as catering stainless steel trays for hotel and buffet service. Catering trays with lids close the transport gap: lidded trays stack in the vehicle, survive the ride to a beach house and keep the food covered until the set up starts.

Chafing dishes:
the hot-food engine.

Buffet service

Chafing dish catering is the standard hot-food service for Philippine buffet lines. Catering chafing dishes are the covered metal warmers behind that line: each holds a single dish over a fuel source, with a water pan underneath and a roll-top lid that keeps heat between refills. A full-size chafer holds one steam table pan and feeds roughly 25 to 30 guests as one hot dish, and a 100-guest buffet with six hot dishes plus backups runs 10 to 14 chafers.

The chafer count is real math, not padding: one dish per chafer per refill cycle, more pans for fast-moving favorites, fewer for sides. If the venue has no kitchen access, the chafers and fuel are what keep the food safe and hot, so a quote without them is not a cheaper quote, it is an incomplete one. Ask how many chafers back up the mains before you compare buffet quotes; that single question separates complete packages from bare ones.

How trays and chafers
move the quote.

No published prices

Trays and chafers are quoted as lines, not as a mystery: the food prices per tray or per head, the equipment prices per piece, and the service prices by the hour when attendants are needed. The lines that move a tray quote:

Tray count and menu fit. A tray that serves 8 to 12 as one dish beside rice stretches further than the same tray standing alone as cocktail food. The caterer sizes the count against the full menu, and the count drives food cost more than any other choice you make.

Material and presentation. Foil and plastic price low and travel light; stainless and silver earn their cost at styled events. A silver tray line-up at a head table is a decor job, and it prices like one.

Chafers, fuel and transport. Chafing dishes, fuel, underliners and serving utensils are often rented per event, and lidded trays or hot boxes appear on the quote when the venue is far. Check that fuel and attendants sit inside the same line so the comparison between caterers stays honest.

Common questions.

Catering trays FAQs

What are catering trays?
Catering trays are the shared food containers a caterer delivers set on your table: large food trays good for 8 to 12 guests, smaller serving and party trays, and the covered chafing dishes that keep a buffet hot. They price below staffed service because guests serve themselves and the caterer plans the count, the transport and the set up instead of assigning servers.

How many people does one catering tray serve?
A full-size catering food tray serves 8 to 12 guests as one shared dish, and a party tray serves 10 to 15 as a snack or dessert course. The honest count depends on what else is on the table: a tray line-up beside rice and viands stretches further than the same trays standing alone as cocktail food. Your caterer sizes the count against the full menu.

What is a chafing dish in catering?
A chafing dish is the covered metal warmer that keeps buffet food at serving temperature: one dish sits in a water pan over a fuel source, and the lid holds heat between refills. Caterers count one chafer per hot dish per refill cycle, which is why the chafer count appears as its own line on a buffet quote.

How many chafing dishes does a 100-guest buffet need?
A 100-guest buffet with six hot dishes plus backups runs 10 to 14 chafing dishes. The math is dishes times refill waves, not guests: fewer, larger pans with scheduled refills beat a long row of half-empty chafers, and the caterer plans the count around the menu and the kitchen access at the venue.

What is the difference between catering trays and chafing dishes?
Catering trays hold and present food at the table, from foil trays at a birthday to silver trays at a head table, and they are part of the delivery and set up. Chafing dishes are equipment that heats: each one holds a single dish over fuel for hours. A buffet quote carries both, the trays for the line and the chafers for the hot dishes on it.

What are catering trays made of?
The common materials are foil for single-event service, food-grade plastic for packed meal drops, stainless steel for the inventory a caterer reuses, and silver or silver plate for styled tables. Material choice follows the job: foil and plastic travel light and price low, stainless and silver earn their cost at styled events and hotel service.

Do caterers rent trays and chafing dishes?
Yes, and the rental line is normal on a quote. Chafing dishes, fuel, underliners and serving utensils are often rented per event, while the food itself is priced per head or per tray. If the quote lists equipment separately, check that fuel and attendants are inside the same line so the comparison between caterers stays honest.
