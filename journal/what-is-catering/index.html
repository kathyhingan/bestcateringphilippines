Catering, Explained

What Is Catering?
Types, terms, and how it works.

A plain-language guide to what catering means, the service types you will be choosing between, and the words caterers use, written for Philippine celebrations.

Catering is the business of preparing and serving food and drinks for an event at a location you choose. A caterer plans the menu with you, cooks or finishes the food, brings the cooking and serving equipment, provides the service staff, feeds your guests, and clears everything away afterward. In the Philippines it covers everything from a 30-person birthday lunch at home to a 500-guest wedding reception in a hotel ballroom.

This guide is the starting point if you are new to planning a celebration and want to understand the words before you start collecting quotes. When you are ready to move forward, the wedding catering page and the birthday catering page cover the two most common occasions in detail.

What does cater mean?

The word itself

To cater means to provide food and service for a gathering. If a company caters your wedding, that company is the one cooking, delivering and serving the meal. In everyday event talk, the caterer is simply the food provider for the party, and catering is the service they provide.

The word also has a wider, older meaning: to supply whatever is needed for a person, group or purpose. That is why you will meet phrases like "cater to guests" or "catered to your needs" in contexts that have nothing to do with food. For event planning, though, the meaning is specific and consistent: food, drinks, and the service around them, provided for your gathering.

Common synonyms and near-synonyms you will see in the Philippines: food catering service, banquet service, food service provider, and events food supplier. They all describe the same trade. A caterer is different from a restaurant because the caterer comes to you, and different from a simple food stall supplier because a caterer brings service staff and event equipment, not just containers of food.

What are the types of catering services?

Know before you compare quotes

Every catering quote you collect will sit somewhere in these types. Knowing the name of each one makes the quotes easier to compare, because two offers at very different prices are often simply different types of service.

On-premise catering

On-premise catering happens in the caterer's own venue, such as a function hall the caterer operates. The kitchen is on site, so food travels the shortest possible distance from stove to table. You are booking the venue and the food together.

Off-premise catering

Off-premise catering is food brought to your chosen venue, whether that is a home, a rented hall, a garden, or a beachfront. The caterer transports the food and equipment and sets up on site. Most home and garden celebrations in the Philippines are off-premise, and it demands more logistics from the caterer, which is part of what a quote pays for.

Full-service catering

Full-service catering means the caterer handles the meal and the service around it: service staff, tables and chairs setup, buffet or plated service during the event, and cleanup afterward. For weddings and large debuts this is the default expectation, and it is the type Best Catering Philippines manages.

Buffet, plated, and food stations

These are the three common service styles inside an event. A buffet lays the dishes out and guests serve themselves, which handles varied appetites well and keeps costs manageable. Plated service brings each course to the seated guest, which feels formal and controls portions but needs more staff per guest. Food stations split the menu into themed counters, such as a carving station or a dessert bar, and guests move between them. Many receptions combine styles, for example a plated main course with a dessert station.

Packed meals and food trays

Packed meals are individually boxed portions, and food trays are large containers meant to be shared across a table or office. Neither comes with event service staff, which makes them the practical choice for office lunches, wakes, Christmastime giveaways, and casual gatherings. They are the most common entry point into ordering catering food in the Philippines.

Mobile catering

Mobile catering is food prepared and sold from a cart, stall or truck that comes to the event. It suits festivals, school fairs, and casual corporate events where guests roam freely rather than sitting to one long meal.

Word note

Why do people say buffer when they mean buffet?

Search data and group chats both show "buffer" used where buffet is meant, so if you have seen "buffer meaning in catering," the answer is simple: it is the same word, the self-serve meal arrangement where dishes are laid out on a table and guests serve themselves. A buffet needs chafing dishes or warmers to hold food at serving temperature through the event.

What this means: when comparing quotes, check whether the buffet line includes the chafing dishes, the fuel, and the buffet attendants, because those three line items are what keep a buffet working for the length of a program.

What does a caterer actually do?

From inquiry to cleanup

The work of a caterer runs in a loop, and knowing the loop tells you what to ask for at each step:

1. Inquiry and quote

You send your event date, guest count, venue, and budget. The caterer replies with a quote, in the Philippines almost always priced per head (per guest), sometimes written as per pax. The quote lists the menu, the service style, the equipment, and the staff.

2. Menu tasting

For weddings and large events, caterers offer a tasting of the proposed menu before you commit. A tasting is where menu promises become real food you have actually eaten, so treat it as part of the vetting.

3. Contract and deposit

Agreed menus are locked in a contract with a reservation deposit. The contract should state the final guest count deadline, because caterers finalize purchasing and staffing around that number.

4. Event day

The caterer delivers or cooks on site, sets the buffet or plates the courses, staffs the service, and coordinates timing with your program. This is the part you will remember, and it is why long programs need a caterer experienced in holding food quality and timing across several hours.

5. Cleanup

After the last course, the caterer packs up equipment, clears the service areas, and leaves the venue as agreed in the contract. Full-service quotes include this; make sure yours does.

Catering in Tagalog: the words Filipinos use.

Speak the language of the booking

In the Philippines, the English word catering is used as is in everyday Tagalog conversation, sometimes spelled katering in casual writing. The common spoken pattern is magpa-cater, to have an event catered, as in "magpa-cater na lang tayo" when a family decides to hire a caterer instead of cooking. There is no single everyday Tagalog verb for "to cater"; speakers borrow the English word.

Related native words you will hear around any handaan:

Handaan is a feast or gathering where food is central, the general word for the celebratory meal itself. Salu-salo is sharing a meal together, which is the spirit a caterer is hired to serve. Paghahain is the serving of food, the act a catering crew performs. Putahe are the dishes, which is what a menu lists. And kusina is the kitchen, which for off-premise catering is temporarily rebuilt at your venue.

When you inquire with a caterer in the Philippines, mixing the two languages is normal and understood everywhere: "Magkano po per head para sa 100 guests, buffet?" is a complete and correct inquiry.

Common questions.

Before you ask

What does catering mean in simple terms?

Catering is the business of preparing and serving food and drinks for an event at a location you choose. A caterer plans the menu, cooks or finishes the food, brings the equipment and staff, serves your guests, and cleans up afterward.

What does the word cater mean?

To cater means to provide food and service for a gathering. In everyday event talk, the caterer is the food provider for the party. The word also has a wider meaning, to supply what is needed, which is why phrases like cater to guests appear outside food contexts.

What is catering called in Tagalog?

Filipinos usually use the English word catering as is, sometimes spelled katering in casual writing, and say magpa-cater to mean having an event catered. Related native words are handaan for a feast or gathering, salu-salo for sharing a meal, and paghahain for the serving of food.

What are the types of catering services?

The common types are on-premise catering in the caterer's own venue, off-premise catering brought to your venue, full-service catering with staff and styling, buffet, plated and food-station service, packed meals and food trays, and mobile catering from food carts or trucks.

How does booking a caterer work in the Philippines?

You send an inquiry with your event date, guest count, venue and budget, the caterer returns a quote priced per head, you agree on a menu, sign a contract and pay a reservation deposit, then the caterer handles equipment, staffing and service on the day itself.
