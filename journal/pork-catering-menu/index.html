Why pork leads
a Filipino table.

Start here

A pork menu for catering in the Philippines is closer to a default than a decision. The roast takes the centerpiece slot at weddings and christenings, the grill covers the casual parties, and the braised pots fill the handaan table at Christmas, so guests read the whole spread by what the pork dish is. Pork is one of the three mains a Filipino buffet is built around, beside chicken and fish, and the dish people remember is usually the pork one.

That makes the real question a planning question: which preparations you order, and how much of each. Two things keep it simple. Pork prices in its own lines, the roast by weight and the viands per head inside the menu, and the portion math is the same math the rest of the table uses.

The rest of this guide walks the three pork families and the dishes inside each, the portions to expect, and how every pork line shows up on a quote, so the pork course can be ordered with everything else in one go.

The three pork families,
dish by dish.

Pick the dish

Filipino pork dishes for catering land in three families, and the family decides how a dish prices more than the recipe does: the roast sells by weight, the grill by the piece or the tray, and the braise per head inside the menu.

The roast. Lechon is the whole roasted pig, the centerpiece that arrives with a carving station and quotes per kilo or per whole pig, separate from the per-head menu. Lechon kawali is pork belly simmered tender then fried crisp in pieces, and crispy pata is the same treatment on the leg: both plate as viands and sit inside the package. For a smaller table, lechon belly rolls a boneless roast that carves like a loin.

The grill. Pork BBQ skewers are the party standard, threaded on sticks and cooked over coals, ordered by the piece or by the tray for cocktails, children's parties and pulutan spreads. Inihaw na liempo and grilled pork chops carry the same smoke to a sit-down buffet, and a pork ribs menu for catering adds the rack side when the grill station carries the program.

The braise. This is the handaan family: pork humba, pork adobo, menudo, mechado, sisig and lechon paksiw. These hold their quality across a long program, price per head inside the menu, and are usually the dishes that stretch a package to more guests for less money.

The working list of pork dishes for catering menus reach for again and again: lechon, lechon kawali, crispy pata, BBQ skewers, humba, adobo, menudo, sisig and ham at Christmas. Most spreads carry one roast, one grilled item and one or two braises, with chicken, fish, vegetables and noodles covering the rest of the table so the pork stays the highlight instead of the whole menu.

Building the pork
catering menu by occasion.

By occasion and format

A pork catering menu follows the occasion first. Weddings and debuts give the roast the centerpiece slot with a carving station. Christenings, birthdays and graduations lean on lechon kawali, BBQ skewers and one braised pot at the family table. Corporate events with a packed meal keep the pork as one viand inside the box, and a plated dinner puts it on the plate as the main course. Christmas and New Year tables add ham, and lechon paksiw earns its place the day after.

Service style moves the same dishes around. A chafing-dish buffet keeps the braises hot and the lechon carved to order. Packed meals put one pork viand in every box. A food station format gives the grill its own counter, which is where BBQ skewers and inihaw plates do their best work. None of these change what pork costs to buy, but they change how it is served and what the quote carries beside it.

How the pork lines
price on a quote.

No published prices

Pork prices three different ways on the same quote, and knowing which is which is what keeps a comparison honest.

The roast, by weight. Lechon quotes per kilo or per whole pig, outside the per-head rate, and the order is sized against the guest count and the rest of the line. Caterers commonly plan one whole lechon of about 20 to 25 kilos for roughly 50 to 80 guests when it shares the table with rice and the other viands, and size up from there when the roast is the main event. Delivery timing and a carving attendant belong to that line, so confirm both when you add it.

The grill, by the piece or the tray. BBQ skewers and inihaw plates price by the stick, the dozen or the tray, which makes them easy to scale for a cocktail hour or a children's party without touching the main menu.

The braises, per head. Humba, adobo, menudo and sisig sit inside the per-head rate like any other viand. What moves their cost is the cut, the portion size and how many viands share the table. Filipino portions are commonly planned at about 250 to 300 grams of combined meat per adult guest across all dishes, so a pork-heavy line usually means fewer main dishes rather than more food.

Common questions.

Pork catering FAQs

What pork dishes are usually on a Filipino catering menu?
The traditional list of Filipino pork dishes for catering services runs lechon as the centerpiece, lechon kawali and crispy pata as crisp viands, pork BBQ skewers for the grill, humba, adobo, menudo, mechado and sisig among the braises, and ham at Christmas. Most menus carry one roast, one grilled item and one or two braised dishes, with the rest of the table left to chicken, fish, vegetables and noodles.

Is lechon included in a per-head catering package?
Usually not. Lechon is quoted by weight, per kilo or per whole pig, as its own line on the quote, because the roast is ordered against its own weight and delivery. Some packages include lechon kawali or crispy pata instead, since those are plated viands that price per head like the rest of the menu. Ask which one your package covers before you assume the centerpiece is inside the rate.

How many kilos of lechon should I order for 100 guests?
Pork orders are sized against the whole line, not the roast alone. Caterers commonly plan one whole lechon of about 20 to 25 kilos for roughly 50 to 80 guests when it shares the table with rice and the other viands, so a 100-guest handaan usually means a larger pig or a second roast, with the final weight confirmed against your guest count and menu.

How much pork per guest should a buffet plan?
Plan about 250 to 300 grams of combined meat per adult guest across all dishes, less when five or more viands plus rice and pasta share the table, because variety substitutes for volume. A menu with lechon kawali, BBQ skewers and one braised pot does not need each dish sized as if it were the only main.

What is the difference between lechon and lechon kawali?
Lechon is the whole roasted pig, ordered by weight and carved at the venue, and it takes the centerpiece slot with a carving station. Lechon kawali is pork belly simmered until tender then fried crisp in pieces, plated as a viand and priced per head inside the menu. One is a production with its own line on the quote, the other is a dish on the package.

Does pork work on a tighter budget?
Yes, and that is where the braises earn their place. Pork humba, adobo and menudo stretch further per peso than a whole roast because they are priced per head and cook in volume, and a tray of BBQ skewers scales up or down by the piece. A budget line usually drops the whole lechon, keeps one crisp pork dish and leans on the braised family for the rest.

Does the pork course change for a wedding or a debut?
The dishes stay close and the format changes. Weddings and debuts usually give the roast the centerpiece slot with a carving station, add a crisp pork starter or a sisig station later in the program, and sometimes carry a second pork dish as a late-night item. The rest of the table follows the occasion, and the package still prices per head with the roast as its own line.
