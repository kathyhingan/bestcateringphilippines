A catering menu is the list of dishes and drinks a caterer will prepare and serve at your event, quoted per head or bundled into a package. In the Philippines it usually means rice, two or three mains, a vegetable dish, pasta or pancit, a dessert and a drink, plus the equipment and staff that put the food on the table. This guide shows you what a catering food menu contains, gives you sample menus for each occasion, and explains how serving styles like buffet lines, food trays and grazing tables change the quote.

How a catering menu is built

Packages vs custom menus

Caterers build menus two ways. A package menu bundles a fixed set of dishes with service staff, equipment and setup into one per-head rate, and it is what most caterers quote first because it is proven, repeatable and easy to compare. A custom menu starts from your own dish list, and the caterer prices it after checking ingredients, prep time and equipment. Custom menus cost more when they need rare ingredients, extra cooks or special equipment, not because the food itself is fancier.

A typical catering menu package in the Philippines includes:

Rice (plain, garlic or paella style), two to three mains (one pork, one chicken or beef, often one seafood), one vegetable dish, pasta or pancit, a dessert, and bottomless iced tea. Weddings add a soup course and a second dessert or a cake cutting service. Handaan-style menus for birthdays and christenings often add lechon or fried chicken. Corporate menus swap in packed meals and coffee service. What is never included by default: venue, tables and chairs from the venue side, and alcohol, which caterers handle only as an add-on or corkage arrangement.

Most caterers offer a menu tasting for weddings and large debuts before you sign. Treat the tasting as a working session: taste the actual dishes at serving temperature, check how the rice holds, and confirm the exact mains against the contract, because substitutions after signing are the number one menu dispute.

Sample catering menus, by occasion

Eight occasions

These are the menu patterns Philippine caterers quote most often, occasion by occasion. Each one links to the full occasion page where the guest counts, program and venue notes live.

Wedding reception menu

The formal sit-down standard: soup, rice, two mains such as beef tenderloin and grilled fish, a vegetable side, pasta, a plated dessert, and bottomless drinks. Buffet weddings swap the plated courses for a wider buffet line, often with a carving station and a dessert station. See wedding catering.

Debut menu

Debuts run close to wedding menus but younger in flavor: a buffet with three mains, a pasta bar, a grazing table or dessert bar for the 18 roses segment, and iced tea or a mocktail station. See 18th debut catering.

Christening or baptism menu

Handaan style after the church rite: pancit for long life, lechon or fried chicken, lumpia, rice, fruit salad, and softdrinks plus iced tea. Often served as shared food trays at home. See christening catering.

Birthday party menu

Kids' parties run on party trays: spaghetti, fried chicken, mini burgers, cupcakes and a cake, with juice. Adult birthdays go handaan style, closer to the christening menu, or buffet for milestone years. See birthday catering.

Graduation menu

A family lunch after the ceremony: rice, one or two mains, pancit, vegetable dish, dessert and drinks, usually as shared trays or a simple buffet at home. See graduation catering.

Corporate event menu

Seminars and office celebrations run on packed meals and food trays with a coffee and snacks station, while year-end parties and client events upgrade to buffets with stations. See corporate event catering.

Christmas party menu

The Filipino Christmas table: ham or lechon, queso de bola, fruit salad, spaghetti, rice and drinks, scaled from office party trays to full family buffets. See Christmas party catering.

New Year menu

Media noche patterns: pancit, lechon or ham, sticky rice dishes for luck, fruit spread of twelve round fruits, and desserts, served family style or as shared trays. See New Year catering.

Serving styles and the equipment behind them

What changes the quote

The same menu prices differently depending on how it is served, because each style carries its own equipment and staffing.

Buffet line

Dishes laid out on long tables, guests serve themselves. The quote carries chafing dishes, fuel, buffet tables, skirting and buffet attendants. A buffet line is the middle of the price range and handles varied appetites well.

Plated service

Each course comes to the seated guest. The quote carries more waiters per guest, plateware and a warmer kitchen rhythm. Plated is the formal option for weddings and costs the most per head of the three sit-down styles.

Food stations

Themed counters, such as a carving station, a pasta bar or a dessert bar, that guests move between. Stations price like buffets plus the styling and any live cooking, and they pair well with debuts and corporate parties.

Grazing tables

A styled spread of cheeses, cold cuts, fruits, breads and sweets that stays open through the event. Grazing table catering is priced per table length or per head, and it doubles as decor, which is why it shows up at debuts, bridal showers and cocktail hours.

Food trays and packed meals

Catering food trays are large shared containers, usually good for 8 to 12 people, delivered set on a table with utensils. Catering packed meals are individually boxed, one per guest. Neither carries service staff, which makes them the most affordable catering menu format in the country and the default for office lunches, seminars, wakes and feeding programs.

Quote note

Why do quotes list chafing dishes separately?

Catering chafing dishes are the covered metal warmers that keep buffet food at serving temperature for hours, each one holding a single dish over a fuel source. Caterers list them as a line item because the count is real math: one dish per chafer per refill cycle, and a 100-guest buffet with six hot dishes plus backups runs 10 to 14 chafers. If a venue has no kitchen access, the chafers and fuel are what keep the food safe and hot, so a quote without them is not a cheaper quote, it is an incomplete one.

What this means: when you compare buffet quotes, check that chafing dishes, fuel and buffet attendants are inside the per-head rate, and ask how many chafers back up the mains. That single question separates complete buffet packages from bare ones.

Popular add-ons

Dessert and pork

Dessert catering

Dessert catering ranges from a simple plated dessert inside the package to a dedicated dessert bar: cakes, cupcakes, kakanin, leche flan, halo-halo stations and candy carts for kids' parties. Wedding dessert bars and candy carts are quoted separately from the menu because they bring their own staff, styling and rental pieces.

Pork menu for catering

Pork anchors most Filipino celebration menus: lechon as the centerpiece with a carving station, pork BBQ skewers for casual parties, lechon kawali and pork humba on handaan buffets, and ham at Christmas. Lechon is usually quoted per kilo or per whole pig separate from the per-head menu, and it needs delivery timing and a carving attendant, so confirm both when you add it.

How caterers price a menu

The quote math

Caterers price per head, and five things move the rate: the service style (packed meals are cheapest, plated is highest), the menu length and ingredients, the equipment the venue demands (no kitchen means more chafers, warmers and transport), the staff ratio (waiters per guest), and the travel distance from the caterer's kitchen to your venue.

Common questions.

Catering menu FAQs

What is usually on a catering menu?

A Philippine catering menu usually centers on rice with two or three mains, one vegetable dish, pasta or noodles, a dessert, and bottomless iced tea. Wedding menus add a soup course and a second dessert, while handaan menus for birthdays and christenings often add lechon or fried chicken and pancit for long life.

What is the difference between food trays and packed meals?

Food trays are large shared containers, usually good for 8 to 12 people, set on a table with serving utensils. Packed meals are individually boxed portions, one per guest. Trays suit office celebrations and family gatherings where people sit and share, packed meals suit seminars, feeding programs and events where guests eat at different times.

What is a grazing table?

A grazing table is a long, styled spread of cheeses, cold cuts, fruits, breads and sweets that guests pick at throughout the event instead of a single sit-down course. It doubles as part of the event styling, which is why it is popular at debuts, bridal showers and corporate cocktail hours.

How many dishes should a catering menu have?

A common package is rice, two to three mains, one vegetable, pasta or pancit, a dessert and a drink, which is about six to eight items per guest. Fewer than that and the line feels thin for a wedding, more than eight usually adds cost without adding much to the guest experience.

How do caterers price their menus?

Caterers price per head, and the rate moves with the service style, the menu length, the equipment the venue needs, the staff ratio and the travel distance. Packages bundle a fixed set of dishes with service and equipment, which is why two quotes with different prices are often simply different packages rather than different value.
