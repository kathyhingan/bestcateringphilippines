By occasion: the types of catering events people book

Examples 1 to 4

The first way to sort the different types of catering is by what you are celebrating. The types of catering events Philippines caterers price most often are few, and each carries its own shape of day.

Example 1: wedding catering. The flagship. A wedding quote usually carries the largest crew, the tightest timeline and the most styling, because the meal runs inside a program that cannot be paused. Receptions of 100 to 200 guests typically land on buffet or plated service, and the inquiry needs the venue named early, because a ballroom wedding and a garden wedding are two different logistics problems. The full decision path lives on the wedding catering page.

Example 2: corporate event catering. Seminars, town halls, product launches and year-end parties. Daytime corporate catering runs on coffee breaks, packed meals or lunch buffets with lean crews, and the invoice goes through finance, so paperwork matters as much as pans. Evening corporate parties look more like weddings without the rites. The corporate event catering page breaks down both.

Example 3: birthday, christening and graduation catering. The family trio. These events run smaller and looser than weddings, home venues are common, and the formats that fit are buffets, family style tables and drop-off trays. A christening lunch of 60 in a garage-turned-function-room is one of the most booked events in the country, and it prices very differently from the same count in a hotel. See birthday catering, christening catering and graduation catering.

Example 4: Christmas and New Year catering. The seasonal spike. December office parties and family reunions compress into the same three weeks, so the caterers who take them book out early and minimums rise with demand. Media noche spreads, ham and queso de bola packages and lechon orders are their own product family. The Christmas catering and New Year catering pages carry the timing rules, and the one that matters most is book in October, not December.

By service style: how the food arrives

Examples 5 to 8

The second sorting is the service style, and it is the sorting that decides which catering best fits your room and your program. The four styles below are the ones Philippine caterers staff for; the format comparison guide covers each in depth, so this is the survey version.

Example 5: buffet service. The national default. Long tables with chafing dishes, guests serving themselves in waves, attendants keeping the line full. A buffet scales from a 30-guest christening to a 500-cover company party on the same basic system, which is why most inquiries land here first. The buffet catering guide walks the line, the layout and the crew.

Example 6: plated service. The restaurant brought to the reception. Guests stay seated, courses fire on a schedule, and the brigade is the largest of any style, often one server per ten to twelve guests. Plated suits formal weddings and award nights where the program runs between courses, and it prices above buffet at the same menu depth because people are the cost.

Example 7: food stations and cocktail style. Carving, pasta, dessert bar, mobile pizza ovens: the meal becomes movement. Stations spread a crowded room, photograph well and turn the cocktail hour into the main event, which is why debuts and launches favor them. Each station is its own staffing unit, so a stations quote should itemize per station, not blend one line.

Example 8: family style and tabletop catering. Platters and boards set directly on the guest tables, passed and refilled by runners. Family style keeps the seated calm of plated service with the abundance of a buffet, and tabletop catering, where every course is arranged on the table itself, is its close cousin. Both suit reunions, debuts and intimate weddings where the table conversation is the program, and both need tables wide enough to carry the spread.

By sector: who runs the kitchen

Examples 9 to 12

The third sorting is the one behind the scenes: the types of catering operations that exist in the market, and who they employ. Knowing them explains why quotes from two companies for the same event can look nothing alike.

Example 9: commercial catering companies. The core market: independent caterers whose main business is events, plus the event arms of restaurants and hotels. This is the group a celebration hires from, and it ranges from family operations running weekends to full production kitchens with fleets and warehouses. When people compare catering companies, food catering companies and event and catering companies in one breath, this is the group they mean, and the honest spread inside it is wide: two local catering companies in the same city can carry very different kitchens, crews and permits behind the same word. Big catering companies and the biggest catering companies win on redundancy (backup equipment, standby crews, multiple trucks), while the most popular catering companies in a city win on taste and repetition at a handful of venues. Best catering companies, in the end, are the ones whose paperwork, tasting and crew commitments survive vetting, which is the whole reason Best Catering Philippines vets before a caterer joins the roster.

Example 10: industrial and institutional catering. The volume end of the trade. Industrial catering examples include factory canteens serving three shifts, school and university feeding programs, hospital dietary departments and office building food service. These are contract operations priced per head per day, measured in consistency rather than celebration, and they are a different business from event work, though many event caterers run a canteen contract to keep kitchens busy between weekends. If your inquiry mentions 300 daily covers, you are quoting this sector, and the math is volume math.

Example 11: club and banquet catering. Kitchen operations attached to a venue: country clubs, golf clubs, condominium function halls and standalone banquet halls. Club catering examples read differently from the rest of this list because the caterer and the venue are the same company, which bundles the room, the food and the rules into one contract. That bundling is convenient and it is also where per-head minimums, corkage fees and exclusive-vendor clauses live, so read them before the date, not after.

Example 12: mobile and food truck catering. The kitchen on wheels. Food trucks, coffee carts, mobile bars and pizza trailers serve festivals, fairs, backyard parties and cocktail hours, and they price per item or per package rather than per head. As an example of catering services built for movement rather than seating, mobile units solve a problem seated formats cannot: they go where the guests already are, and they carry their own power.

One textbook note ties this section together: the types of catering establishment the hospitality books list (commercial, institutional, club and banquet, mobile) are exactly the four groups above, and asking which one you are talking to is a legitimate question for any shortlisted caterer, because it predicts their pricing structure before the quote arrives.

The 2 types of catering underneath every example

The split that decides responsibility

Search what are the 2 types of catering and every source gives the same split: on premise and off premise. Search what are the types of catering and the twelve examples above are the honest answer, sorted by occasion, style and sector. The on premise and off premise split is the one sorting that sits under all twelve examples, because it decides who owns the kitchen, the power and the risk.

On premise. The food is produced and served inside the caterer's own facility: hotel ballrooms with banquet kitchens downstairs, restaurant event rooms, clubhouse kitchens with resident crews. Control is the advantage, since distances are short and backup equipment sits on a shelf downstairs. The trade is that you inherit the house menu, the house minimums and the house corkage rules.

Off premise. The kitchen travels. Tents, generators, mobile kitchens and crews arrive at your garden, beach, home or bare function hall, and every logistical line (power, water, weather cover, load-in) becomes part of the quote. Freedom of venue and menu is the advantage; logistics is the price.

Search the 4 types of catering instead and the answer changes register: buffet, plated, family style and food stations, the four service styles from the section above. The two answers are not rivals. The two types of catering, on premise and off premise, describe where the kitchen stands; the two types of catering service questions you will actually field from a caterer (style and venue) describe what the guests experience and where they sit. A useful vocabulary check when comparing quotes: ask which types of catering system the company runs for your event, a resident kitchen or a traveling brigade, and the fine print of the quote starts writing itself. Samples, quotes and small parties

From example to decision

Small parties need their own note, because the advice above assumes a staffed event. The best catering for small parties is the format that does not overstaff the room: under about 30 guests, drop-off trays, packed meals or a lean single-line setup beat a full brigade on price, and the best food to cater for a party of that size is food that holds well without constant attention (noodle bases, braises, grilled items over delicate plating). From about 30 guests up, a small buffet or family style table earns its crew, and the best party catering question resolves itself into headcount and venue, menu second.

Types and examples FAQs

What are the two types of catering?

The two types of catering are on premise and off premise. On premise means the food is produced and served inside the caterer's own facility, like a hotel ballroom with a banquet kitchen downstairs. Off premise means the kitchen travels to your venue with its equipment, power and crew. The four service styles (buffet, plated, family style and food stations) run on top of either type, which is why venue and style are two separate decisions.

What are the types of catering services?

The types of catering services Philippine clients book sort into three groups: by occasion (weddings, corporate events, birthdays and family celebrations, Christmas and New Year), by service style (buffet, plated, food stations and cocktail, family style and tabletop), and by sector (commercial companies, industrial and institutional operations, club and banquet kitchens, mobile units). The twelve examples in this guide walk each group with what it includes and where it fits.

What are the different types of catering establishments?

The different types of catering establishments split into four groups: commercial caterers whose main business is events, institutional operations that feed canteens in schools, hospitals and offices, club and banquet operations attached to a venue, and mobile units such as food trucks and trailers. For a one-day celebration you will usually hire from the first group, but a venue with its own banquet operation quietly moves you into the third, along with that venue's menu rules and minimums.
