The catering equipment list, section by section

The checklist

Use this section as a catering equipment checklist and as the catering tools and equipment list to hand anyone quoting you, and tick it against any quote you receive. Every event runs on the same six groups, whatever the occasion, and the groups fail in order: no power means no heat, no heat means a cold line, no transport means the first two never arrive.

Cooking and holding. Chafing dishes, steam tables, soup warmers and kettles, butane and gas burners, roasting ovens for on-site work. This is the group that keeps the Philippine buffet alive for a four-hour reception, and it is where a caterer's investment shows first. Home-party hosts mostly skip this group by ordering trays, which is why drop-off food pricing looks cheap and tastes it.

Serving. The serving equipment for catering lines: buffet frames and risers, serving trays, plates and bowls, drink dispensers, coffee stations. Presentation lives here, and so does queue speed, because a line with too few serving points backs up no matter how good the food is. The setup and styling guide covers how the line is arranged and dressed.

Smallwares and utensils. Tongs, ladles, serving spoons, cake knives, gloves, the unglamorous tools that move food from container to plate. Catering utensils are cheap to buy and impossible to improvise on the day, and catering equipment and utensils travel together: machines without smallwares serve nothing, so a shortlist that forgets them is a shortlist with a problem.

Transport. Insulated carriers, thermal boxes, beverage coolers, dollies. Philippine events happen across cities, up mountainsides and over water, and the carrier is the difference between food that arrives at temperature and food that arrives with a story. Island venues, Boracay the famous case, route every tray through a jetty crossing, which is why their quotes carry logistics lines.

Power and shelter. Generators, extension and distribution, tents, sidewalls, lighting. Outdoor celebrations in the Philippines run on this group: a garden reception without generator backup is a weather bet, and December is the wrong month to lose it. Tents are rented per frame, so this is the group that grows fastest when the venue is bare.

Furniture. Guest tables and chairs, buffet tables, linens, skirting. Chairs and tables are the most-rented items in the entire events industry, and the cheapest place to spot a padded quote: furniture lines that duplicate what the venue already provides.

Searches for a catering equipment list pdf are really asking for this section printable, and the honest version is the six groups plus the headcount rules below. Print the page, tick the groups, and any quote that leaves a group unpriced deserves a question.

Catering food warmers, the load-bearing gear

The 720-search question

Food warmers are the load-bearing group of food warmer catering equipment, the most-searched corner of the whole list, and for good reason: they are the piece that holds the buffet together. When people search catering food warmers they are comparing three machines that look alike and behave differently, so here is the plain version.

Chafing dishes. The classic catering warmers: a water pan, a food pan above it, a fuel burner or electric element below. A full-size chafer holds a single viand for 50 to 60 portions and keeps it at serving temperature for hours. They are the default on Philippine buffet lines because they are stackable, rentable by the piece and replaceable mid-event. Check the gauge before renting: thin pans warp, and a warped pan is a spill on a crowded line.

Steam tables. The commercial catering equipment answer for volume: a plumbed or electric table that holds multiple pans at controlled temperature, standard in hotel banquet kitchens and canteens. Steam tables do not travel well, so they appear on quotes where the venue has a real kitchen behind the line. If your venue is a garden, the quote should say chafers, not steam tables.

Soup warmers and kettles. Rounded, thermostatic, made for liquids. Soup, sauces, and the Filipino stews that sit closest to a Filipino heart. A kettle that holds sinigang at temperature for three hours is worth more than its rental line suggests.

The count rule from the FAQ bears repeating in the body: one full-size warmer per 25 to 30 guests per viand wave, five to eight pieces on a 100-guest line with soup and rice stations counted separately. Add fuel backup of at least one full cycle, because a chafer that dies at hour two takes its viand with it. The menu guide decides how many viands the line carries, and the viand count drives the warmer count, which is why menu and equipment are one decision priced in two lines. Even the buying queries, a food warmer for catering for sale at the appliance mall, resolve to the same three machines, and the same gauge check applies before any of them is rented.

Utensils and smallwares, where service is won

The unglamorous group

Catering utensils decide service speed more than any machine, because every portion passes through a pair of them. The working set for a buffet line: two serving utensils per viand (one to hold, one to plate), ladles sized to the soup and the sauces, tongs for lechon and grills, cake and pie knives for dessert, and enough serving spoons in reserve that a dropped one never stops the line. Searches for serving utensils for catering usually end in restaurant supply shops, and the buying rule is dull and correct: buy more than the line needs, in matching sets, because mixed smallwares photograph badly and serve worse. Rental houses stock catering utensils for rent by the set and supply shops run catering utensils for sale, metal and heavy-duty plastic both.

Utensils for catering services are also the cheapest vetting tell there is. A caterer whose tasting setup arrives with bent tongs and unlabeled serving spoons is showing you the kitchen, and the kitchen is the product. The catering utensils list below doubles as the tasting-day checklist: serving spoons, tongs, ladles, cake knives, gloves, sanitizer, and a food thermometer, which no serious operation serves without.

Food catering utensils for home parties deserve their own note: the disposable aisle has improved, and a family celebration of 60 can run happily on disposable catering supplies with metal only for the heads table. The trade is waste and feel, and the honest midpoint many reunions land on is disposables for plates and cutlery, metal for serving.

Rent or buy: the rental math for one-day events

The 110-search decision

The biggest search cluster in this whole topic is rental: catering equipment rental, catering equipment rental philippines, catering needs for rent, catering rentals, catering services rental, catering supplies rental. Philippine events run on rentals, and the reason is arithmetic. A decent full-size chafer costs several thousand pesos to buy; it rents for a few hundred a day. A thirty-piece chair set for one reunion would spend 364 days a year in storage. For a one-day event, renting wins on cost, on storage, on maintenance and on the failure clause: the rental house swaps a dead burner, the owner replaces it.

The rental market answers every scale of catering needs for rent: chafing dishes, steam tables, hot cases, buffet frames, gazebos and tents, generators, tables and chairs by the hundred, and the specialty pieces (lechon carousels, coffee carts, soft-serve machines) that make debuts and launches photograph well. Catering materials for rent list out the same way on every rental quote: item, quantity, days, delivery and pickup fees. Read the pickup fee twice, because Sunday-night pickups after an all-day event are where surprise charges live. A full catering equipment rental service delivers, installs and swaps failed pieces mid-event, some outfits present as catering and rental services in one, chairs plus warmers plus crew on one invoice, and marketplace listings of catering things for sale read like the same rental catalog without the prices.

Furniture deserves its own numbers. Catering chairs and tables for rent price per piece per day and are the cheapest line to buy wrong: reserve guest chairs at a count that matches the confirmed headcount, not the invitation list, and you save a real line on the invoice. The sale side of the same market, catering chairs for sale, catering tables for sale, round catering tables for sale, catering chairs and tables for sale as one bundle, belongs to venues and recurring organizers, not to one-off hosts, and the used catering equipment for sale listings are where new catering businesses kit out their first kitchen.

Which raises the buying path properly, because three related searches travel together: 2nd hand catering equipment, second hand catering equipment and used catering equipment. For a host, none of it makes sense; renting is cheaper than storing. For a caterer starting out, used commercial catering equipment from a kitchen that is upgrading is the standard entry path, and the inspection rule is simple: burners, thermostats and hinges, tested live before money moves. New catering equipment for sale from a restaurant supply store prices far higher and buys warranty and gauge, which matters for pieces that hold food at temperature all day.

Two rental searches deserve honest framing. Catering kitchens for rent means commissary space: licensed kitchens rented by the hour or month, the way small catering businesses legally operate before they can afford their own. And catering van rental means the vehicle, not the venue, the insulated van that moves a hundred covers across a city without a temperature story. Hosts meet neither; caterers meet both in their first month.

Common questions.

Equipment and supplies FAQs

What equipment is needed for catering?

The equipment needed for catering sorts into six groups: cooking and holding gear such as chafing dishes, steam tables and burners; serving equipment for catering lines, trays and stations; smallwares and utensils such as tongs, ladles and service spoons; transport gear such as insulated carriers and thermal boxes; power and shelter such as generators, tents and lighting; and furniture such as tables, chairs and linens. A full-service caterer brings the first three groups as a matter of course, and quotes the rest as rentals when the venue is bare.

What is the list of catering tools and equipment and their uses?

The classic list of catering tools and equipment and their uses reads like this: chafing dishes keep viands at serving temperature on a buffet line, steam tables hold larger volumes for long service, soup warmers and kettles hold liquids, tongs and ladles and serving spoons move food hygienically, insulated carriers and thermal boxes hold temperature in transit, generators feed power where venues have none, and tents with sidewalls shelter the line from sun and rain. Each piece exists because a celebration serves many guests over several hours, which home kitchens are never built to do.

How many food warmers do I need for 100 guests?

For a 100-guest buffet, the working rule is one full-size chafing dish per 25 to 30 guests per viand wave, so four to six dishes on the line at once, plus a soup station and a rice station of their own. If the menu carries five viands, a 100-guest line usually runs five to eight warmers in total so the line can be refilled without a gap. Count service time into the math: a four-hour reception eats through more refills than a one-hour lunch, and the caterer should size warmers and backup fuel to the longest service window, not the shortest.

Is it better to rent or buy catering equipment?

For a one-day celebration, renting wins almost every time, because catering equipment is expensive to buy, heavy to store and wasteful to own for a single event. Renting also transfers the maintenance risk: the rental company replaces a failed burner, not you. Buying starts to make sense for businesses that cater weekly, and even then the usual path is second hand catering equipment bought from a kitchen that is upgrading, inspected burner by burner. For hosts, the honest answer is that rental prices are a rounding error against the food budget, and the line looks and serves better when the gear matches the event.

What is the difference between catering equipment and catering supplies?

Catering equipment is the durable hardware: chafing dishes, steam tables, utensils, carriers, generators, tables. Catering supplies are the consumables an event burns through: chafing fuel, foil, gloves, garbage bags, disposable catering supplies like plates and cutlery, and packaging for drop-off orders. Equipment is rented or owned across many events, supplies are bought fresh for each one. On a quote, equipment shows up as a rental line and supplies hide inside the service fee, which is one more reason to ask what the fee actually covers.

What does a caterer provide and what do I rent separately?

A full-service caterer provides the cooking and holding equipment, the serving utensils, the crew and usually the buffet tables and linens, because the service depends on them. What you typically rent separately is venue-side: chairs and tables beyond the buffet line, tents and sidewalls for outdoor venues, generators where the venue has no power, and specialty pieces like a lechon station or a coffee cart. Bare venues, beach fronts and private gardens are where the separate rentals grow, so the venue choice should be settled before the quote is finalized.

Where can I buy catering supplies in the Philippines?

For consumables and smallwares, Divisoria in Manila is the classic bulk run, with restaurant supply streets in every major city close behind. Wholesale catering suppliers serve registered businesses by catalog, and restaurant equipment stores in metro areas carry the durable pieces. For a home celebration, disposable catering supplies from restaurant supply shops or online sellers cover the party without a single rental. Check build quality before buying anything that heats: thin-gauge chafing pans warp and spill, and a fuel holder that sits loose is a hazard on a crowded line.
