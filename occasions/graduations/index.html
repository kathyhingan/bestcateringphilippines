The real risk

Filipino graduation celebrations compress an entire extended family's pride into one afternoon: parents, grandparents, titos, titas, and the barkada, often arriving within the same hour after the ceremony itself. March and April are the peak months, which means venues and caterers alike are stretched thin exactly when demand is highest.

A buffet-style setup is the usual choice because it lets a mixed-generation crowd serve themselves at their own pace, but that only works if the caterer has actually planned for a fast-arriving crowd. An underprepared buffet line during peak graduation season means a queue that eats into the time meant for photos and speeches.

We vet for caterers who know how to run a buffet at pace during the busiest weeks of the year, not just on a quiet weekday.

High school and college
graduations aren't the same party.

By graduation level
Grade School & High School

Moving-Up & HS Graduation

High school and grade-school graduation parties function like open houses: a large mix of family, neighbors, classmates and even teachers, with guests dropping by at different times rather than sitting through a single seated program. This is casual and inclusive by nature, and DepEd guidance itself asks schools to keep the ceremony simple, without excessive spending, which sets the tone for the after-party too.

The typical spread is comfort food built to move fast: pancit, spaghetti, fried chicken, sandwiches, a sheet cake, all served buffet-style since guests arrive and leave on their own schedule rather than at one fixed seating.

Catering watch-out: Since guests trickle in over hours rather than arriving together, ask your caterer how they keep a buffet looking fresh and fully stocked across an extended open-house window, not just for a single two-hour service.
College & University

College Graduation

College graduation celebrations are typically smaller and more curated: close family, college friends, and a short list from the old high school circle, often as a dinner, a cocktail hour, or a reserved restaurant section rather than a large open house. Many graduates celebrate away from their hometown, near the university itself, which shifts venue and catering logistics away from the family's usual local vendors.

The food and format usually step up in formality too, a catered sit-down meal or a curated buffet, cocktails instead of just soda, reflecting the transition into early adulthood rather than a childhood milestone.

Catering watch-out: If the celebration is happening near a university city rather than the family's hometown, confirm your caterer actually serves that specific area, since your usual family caterer may not have coverage there.

Common questions.

Before you ask

When should I book graduation catering?

Book as early as possible if your celebration falls in March or April, the peak graduation season nationwide, when both venues and caterers are in highest demand. Requesting a quote early gives you access to more of our vetted bench for your date.

What food works best for a graduation party?

Buffet-style Filipino spreads are the standard choice: lechon or roast pork, chicken, a beef dish, pancit, rice and dessert, because they let a mixed-age crowd of relatives and friends serve themselves without a fixed seating plan. Finger foods and grazing-table formats are also popular for more casual celebrations.

How many guests come to a typical graduation party?

It varies widely with family size, from an intimate 20 to 30 up to 100 or more when extended family and the graduate's full friend group attend. We manage graduation celebrations at 50 to 100+ guests.

Can catering handle a surprise graduation party?

Yes, this is a common format, family and caterer prepare while the graduate is still at the ceremony, so everything is ready the moment they arrive. Tell us this is a surprise when you request your quote so timing is planned around it.
