The real risk

A wedding reception runs on a fixed sequence that cannot be paused: entrance, first dance, speeches, cake cutting, all timed around when the food is actually served. A late buffet or an understaffed plated service does not just delay dinner, it delays the entire program in front of every guest who traveled for the day.

This is the occasion where an unverified caterer is the highest-stakes gamble on the entire site. There is no second wedding day. A caterer who underestimates a 150-guest crowd, runs out of a main course, or sends half the promised waitstaff turns the most photographed meal of your life into the thing your relatives actually talk about afterward.

We vet for capacity at your specific guest count before you ever sign anything, not after a deposit is already gone.

By wedding type
Beach & Destination

Beach Weddings

A beach wedding trades a controlled ballroom for sand, wind, heat and tide, and the catering plan has to change with it. Hot dishes need insulated holding, not just a chafing dish, because open-air heat and sea breeze pull temperature down fast. Ice for drinks needs sealed, separate storage so it does not spoil in the sun.

Setup and load-in usually happen over sand, which means no rolling carts and a longer carry time from the parking area to the reception point. Local government or resort permits often restrict open flame near vegetation, which affects chafing fuel and any live cooking stations.

Catering watch-out: Confirm your caterer has actually worked a beach setup before, not just an outdoor garden. Ask specifically how they hold hot food temperature in direct heat, and what their wind and rain contingency is.
Civil Ceremony

Civil Weddings

A civil wedding is legally identical to a church wedding but usually much smaller and faster to arrange, often 2 to 4 weeks of lead time instead of 6 to 12 months. Receptions frequently move to a restaurant private room or an intimate home setup rather than a full banquet hall.

Catering for a civil wedding is proportionally smaller too: a set restaurant menu or a light catered lunch for 10 to 40 guests is common, a different scale entirely from a 150-guest banquet.

Catering watch-out: Because the lead time is short, ask your caterer directly whether they can turn around a quality menu on a 2 to 4 week timeline. Not every vendor built for a 6-month-planned wedding can move that fast.
Church Ceremony

Church Weddings

A Catholic church wedding requires 6 to 12 months of lead time for banns, Pre-Cana seminars and parish booking, which gives you more runway to plan catering properly, but also means your date is locked in early and your caterer needs to hold it that far out.

Church weddings skew larger and more formal: 100 to 150+ guests is typical, with a reception at a separate hotel ballroom, garden venue or function hall. The gap between ceremony end time and reception start needs a caterer who can have food fully staged and ready the moment guests arrive.

Catering watch-out: Ask whether your caterer requires a deposit to hold a date this far in advance, and what their policy is if your final guest count shifts in the months between booking and the wedding itself.

What each place actually needs
Calabarzon

Tagaytay

Tagaytay's ridge-top elevation means genuinely cooler weather than Manila, often 5 to 8 degrees lower, plus real fog risk especially in the mornings and during rainy months. Garden venues built into the hillside look spectacular but sit on slopes, which changes buffet table placement and can slow food-carrying staff.

Catering watch-out: Ask how your caterer plans for fog delaying photo timelines, which can push your actual meal service later than scheduled, and whether hot food holds through that delay.
Calabarzon

Batangas (Nasugbu, Laiya)

Batangas beach venues run genuinely hot, March to May afternoon temperatures reach the mid-30s Celsius on open sand, and afternoon winds pick up along the coast. Most weddings here are resort-package deals bundling venue and catering together.

Catering watch-out: If you are booking an independent caterer instead of the resort's own package, confirm they have specifically worked Batangas's open coastal heat, not just a shaded garden elsewhere.
Destination Island

Boracay

Boracay has no bridge and no shortcut: every guest and every ingredient crosses by boat from Caticlan, a journey that stops running in bad weather. Resort in-house catering is common and bundled with the venue; an independent caterer needs its own logistics plan for getting food and staff across that water crossing reliably.

Catering watch-out: Ask directly how your caterer handles the boat crossing for both staff and equipment, and what happens to your timeline if weather delays the crossing on your date.
Destination Island

Palawan

Palawan (Puerto Princesa, El Nido) is a genuine fly-in destination for most guests and most suppliers, which means equipment and sometimes ingredients travel to the island specifically for your date. Fewer local catering options exist compared to Manila or Cebu, so lead time matters more here than almost anywhere else on this list.

Catering watch-out: Book meaningfully earlier than a mainland wedding, and confirm your caterer's contingency if a delivery or a flight is delayed.

Common questions.

Before you ask

How much does wedding catering cost in the Philippines?

Per-head rates generally run from about 700 to 1,000 pesos for a basic buffet, 1,000 to 1,800 pesos for a mid-range buffet or plated menu, and 1,800 pesos and up for a premium plated service with full styling. For a 150-guest wedding, mid-range catering alone typically totals around 180,000 to 225,000 pesos. We do not publish fixed prices because your actual quote depends on your venue, menu and guest count. These are general market ranges to help you plan before requesting a quote.

Buffet or plated, which is better for a wedding?

Buffet suits larger, more casual receptions and gives guests menu choice; plated suits formal, seated receptions and needs a higher staff-to-guest ratio. Both are available across our vetted bench; which one fits depends on your venue, your program length and your guest count.

What questions should I ask a wedding caterer?

Ask what is included in the per-head rate (service staff, tables, linens, china), whether the price is VAT-inclusive, the waiter-to-table ratio, what happens if guest count changes close to the date, and what their backup plan is if a staff member or ingredient falls through. Our vetting process checks these before a caterer joins our bench.

How far in advance should I book wedding catering?

Most established caterers in the Philippines need weeks of lead time, and peak wedding months (especially December and the summer months) book out further ahead. The earlier you request a quote, the more of our vetted bench is still available for your date.
