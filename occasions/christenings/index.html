The real risk

A christening is usually a smaller, warmer gathering than a wedding, but the social stakes are not lower: ninongs and ninangs, grandparents from both sides, and relatives who came specifically to witness a milestone. An undersized or oddly-timed spread reads as a slight in a room built entirely around family warmth.

The occasion also has a built-in complication weddings do not: the guest of honor is an infant, which means the event timing often bends around feeding schedules and nap times, and the caterer needs to be ready to serve whenever the family actually calls for it, not on a fixed clock.

We match christening bookings with caterers experienced in family-style service and flexible timing, not rigid banquet-hall schedules.

Home celebration, or
a booked venue.

By setting
Home-Based

Family Home Celebration

Many Filipino christenings happen at the family home, especially for smaller guest lists, which keeps the day intimate but puts real constraints on a caterer: a residential kitchen for staging, limited parking for a catering vehicle, and setup happening around a family's actual living space rather than an empty function room.

The upside is flexibility: timing can bend around the baby's actual schedule far more easily at home than in a rented hall with a fixed time block.

Catering watch-out: Confirm your caterer has residential-setup experience, not just banquet-hall service, and ask how much space and lead time they need to stage a home-based buffet.
Booked Venue

Function Hall or Restored Venue

Larger christenings, especially ones combining both extended families and a full sponsor list, move to a rented function hall or, in some cities, a restored heritage-style venue for a more distinctive backdrop. These venues come with a fixed time block, which removes the home setting's scheduling flexibility but gives the caterer a proper kitchen staging area and loading access.

This setting suits the higher end of the 50 to 100+ guest range where a home simply could not comfortably hold everyone.

Catering watch-out: Fixed-hour venue bookings mean your caterer needs to work around the baby's schedule within that window, not on their own timeline. Confirm this is built into your service plan, since christenings are the one occasion where the guest of honor's nap time can genuinely shift the program.

Common questions.

Before you ask

What is usually included in a christening catering package?

A typical package includes a buffet with a choice of main courses (commonly three to five), rice, a dessert, and refillable drinks, plus basic buffet setup, tables, chairs and service staff. Some packages add a backdrop, balloon styling or a dedicated cake and cupcake table. We do not publish fixed prices; every package is quoted to your guest count and menu.

How many guests attend a typical Filipino christening?

Christening receptions vary widely, from an intimate 30 to 50 relatives up to 100 or more when both extended families and a full sponsor list are involved. We manage christenings at 50 to 100+ guests specifically.

What food is traditional for a Filipino christening?

Classic Filipino buffet dishes are standard: lechon or roast pork, chicken, a beef dish such as caldereta, pancit, rice, and a dessert like leche flan or fruit salad. Caterers on our bench build menus around these staples with room to customize.

Can the caterer work around a baby's feeding and nap schedule?

This is part of what we vet for on this occasion specifically. We match christening bookings with caterers who have experience keeping service flexible around an infant's actual schedule rather than a fixed banquet timeline.
