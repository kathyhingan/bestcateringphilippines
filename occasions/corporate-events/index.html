The real risk

When a family event's catering goes wrong, the embarrassment is real but it stays inside the family. When a corporate event's catering goes wrong, the person who booked it answers to a manager, a client, or a board the next morning. A late buffet at a product launch, a menu that does not account for visiting clients, a seminar lunch that runs out mid-session: these are reputational and professional risks, not just social ones.

That changes what actually matters in a corporate caterer. It is less about sentiment and more about reliability under a tight schedule: a training session that must restart on time, a board dinner where the company's standards are visibly on display, a client lunch where running short of food reflects on the host personally.

We vet corporate caterers specifically for schedule discipline and professional presentation, the things that protect the person who made the booking.

By event format
Structured Schedule

Seminars & Training

Seminar and training catering runs against a locked agenda: a session cannot restart because lunch was late. Packed meals or a fast, organized buffet during a defined break window matter more here than menu variety, since the entire point is minimal disruption to a structured day.

Half-day, full-day and full-board formats each need different timing: a full-day session typically needs a morning snack, lunch, and an afternoon snack all synced to break times set by the agenda, not the caterer's convenience.

Catering watch-out: Confirm your caterer can commit to exact serving windows tied to your agenda's break times, and ask what happens if the session runs over.
Client-Facing

Launches & Networking Events

A product launch or client networking event is judged on presentation as much as food quality, since external guests, clients or press are forming an impression of the company itself. Cocktail-style catering with finger foods and an elegant presentation usually fits better than a heavy buffet, since the format encourages mingling and movement rather than sitting down to eat.

Timing here serves the program: catering needs to be visible and available throughout, not concentrated in one serving window, since guests arrive and mingle at their own pace.

Catering watch-out: Ask your caterer about presentation standards specifically, plating, service staff uniforms, table styling, since this reflects on the host company's brand in front of external guests.
Internal

Company Anniversaries & Team Celebrations

Internal celebrations, company anniversaries, team milestones, year-end recognition events, run closer to a family celebration in tone: a full buffet, a relaxed program, speeches and awards rather than strict business formality. These events can afford a longer, more generous meal service since there is no external audience to manage.

Guest counts here scale with headcount, sometimes reaching well past 100 for a full-company event, which shifts the catering conversation toward the same large-scale logistics a wedding or milestone birthday needs.

Catering watch-out: For company-wide events, confirm your caterer's actual capacity at your full headcount, the same vetting that matters for a large wedding applies here.

Common questions.

Before you ask

What types of corporate catering are there?

The main formats are seminar and training catering (built around a tight schedule, often half-day or full-day), packed meals (individually portioned, no buffet setup needed), buffet catering (for company anniversaries and larger celebrations), and cocktail catering (finger foods for networking events and launches). Tell us your format when you request a quote.

How much does corporate catering cost in the Philippines?

General market rates for regular corporate events run roughly 345 to 1,250 pesos per person depending on package and inclusions, while seminar and training catering typically runs 450 to 950 pesos per person for half-day to full-board options. We do not publish fixed prices; every quote is custom to your event format and headcount.

How far ahead should we book corporate catering?

Most providers recommend booking one to two months ahead for standard corporate events, and as early as possible during peak seasons. The earlier you request a quote, the more of our vetted bench is available for your date.

Can you handle a strict schedule, like a training session with timed breaks?

Yes, this is specifically what we vet corporate caterers for. Seminar and training catering is built around punctual, organized service that does not disrupt a structured agenda.
